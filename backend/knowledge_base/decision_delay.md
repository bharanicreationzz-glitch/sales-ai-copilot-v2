# Decision Delay Handling

knowledge_type: decision_delay

## Principle

DECISION DELAY != CUSTOMER REJECTION

Goal:
Accelerate decision clarity, not force a decision.

Flow:
OBJECTION -> DIAGNOSE -> CLARIFY -> RESOLVE -> CONFIRM -> NEXT STEP

## Common delay signals

- "I'll think about it."
- "Not now."
- "Call later."
- "I need more time."
- "I'll discuss and tell you."
- "I'm comparing."
- "Let me check with my contractor."
- "Let me check with my family."
- "I'll decide next month."

These signals are ambiguous until the barrier is understood.

## Identify the barrier

Possible barriers:
- Price
- Product
- Trust
- Timing
- Approval
- Comparison
- Information gap
- Installation
- Budget

Ask one diagnostic question rather than overwhelming the customer.

Example:
"Before you decide, may I understand what you'd like to consider — price, product or installation?"

## Reduce decision friction

Give:
1. Clear recommendation
2. One or two suitable options
3. Key difference
4. Relevant scope
5. Missing information
6. One next action

Avoid:
- Too many products
- Too many technical details
- Repeating the complete pitch
- Multiple competing calls-to-action

Example:
"Based on your requirement, I would shortlist these two options. The main difference is X. If your priority is Y, I'd recommend A. Shall I prepare the quotation for A?"

## Ethical urgency

Good:
"When are you planning to start the fencing work?"

Good:
"Is there a date by which you need the boundary completed?"

Bad:
"Price will definitely increase tomorrow" when unverified.

Bad:
"Last piece available" when untrue.

Bad:
"You must decide today" without a legitimate reason.

Never fabricate scarcity or urgency.

## Decision-maker

Ask naturally:
"Will you be taking the final decision, or will someone else also be involved?"

If another decision-maker exists:
- Identify their concerns.
- Prepare information for them.
- Offer a joint conversation when appropriate.
- Do not bypass them.

## Multi-call decision delay

If the same lead delays across calls, use the call history.

Example:
Call 1: Interested.
Call 2: Comparing.
Call 3: "I'll think about it."
Call 4: Still undecided.

Coach:
"The customer has already received product information. Focus on identifying the unresolved concern rather than repeating the general pitch."

## Repeated objection

If price appears in multiple calls:
Classify as REPEATED_PRICE_OBJECTION.

Recommended:
"Ask what competitor specification or total project scope is being compared and conduct a like-for-like comparison."

Do not simply repeat the same discount or pitch.
