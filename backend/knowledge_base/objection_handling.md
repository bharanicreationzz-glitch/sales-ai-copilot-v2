# Objection Handling

knowledge_type: objection_handling

## Master framework

ACKNOWLEDGE -> DIAGNOSE -> CLARIFY -> RESPOND -> CONFIRM -> NEXT STEP

The goal is to identify and resolve the customer's real barrier, not to pressure the customer.

## "I'm not interested"

Possible meanings:
- Genuine rejection
- Price concern
- Product concern
- Timing
- Trust
- Information gap
- Competitor selection
- Decision-maker issue

Recommended:
"Sure. May I know what is holding you back — the price, the product, or the timing?"

Do not immediately end with:
"Okay sir, no problem."

If the customer gives a genuine final rejection, respect it.

## "Price is high"

Recommended:
"I understand. May I know what price you're comparing it with? Then I can check whether we're comparing the same specification and installation scope."

If budget is fixed:
"What approximate budget are you trying to stay within? I can then check which suitable option fits that range."

Avoid:
- Immediate discount
- Arguing
- "This is the best price"
- Criticizing competitors

## "I'll think about it"

Possible reasons:
- Price uncertainty
- Product uncertainty
- Need approval
- Comparing suppliers
- Technical information gap
- Installation concern
- Timing
- Budget

Recommended:
"Absolutely. Before you decide, may I understand what you'd like to consider — the price, product or installation?"

## "I'll call you later"

Recommended:
"Sure. Just so I don't disturb you unnecessarily, when would be a convenient time for me to follow up?"

Alternative:
"Would it be better if I send the details on WhatsApp and reconnect once you've had a chance to review them?"

## "Not now"

Recommended:
"Sure. Roughly when are you expecting to start the fencing work?"

If a future date is given, follow up near that timeline rather than repeatedly calling.

## "I need more time"

Recommended:
"Of course. Is there any particular information you still need before you can decide?"

## "I need to discuss with my husband/wife"

Recommended:
"Absolutely. Is there anything specific they would want to know — price, product, installation or appearance?"

If appropriate:
"Would it be useful to have a quick discussion together so I can explain the options directly?"

Do not try to bypass the decision-maker.

## "I need to ask my contractor"

Recommended:
"Sure. What information does your contractor need from us? I can share the product specifications and quotation details."

## "I'm comparing with another supplier"

Recommended:
"What are the main factors you're comparing — price, quality, durability, installation or warranty?"

Do not say:
"Our product is better."

Do not attack competitors.

## "Competitor is cheaper"

Recommended:
"Before comparing only the price, can we check whether the material, height, specification and installation scope are the same?"

Compare like-for-like:
- Material
- Specification
- Height
- Quantity
- Coating
- Accessories
- Installation
- Warranty where applicable
- Delivery/freight
- GST
- Overall scope

## Quality concern

Do not say only:
"Yes, very good quality."

Instead explain relevant factual:
- Material
- Construction
- Coating
- Specification
- Warranty where applicable
- Application

Then:
"What type of usage are you planning? I can explain whether this specification is suitable."

## Installation concern

First establish:
- Location
- Boundary
- Height
- Site condition
- Product

Explain only confirmed installation capability/process.

## Trust concern

Use factual proof:
- Product specifications
- Warranty where applicable
- Professional project handling
- Transparent communication
- Site visit where appropriate

Never invent certifications, warranties, project counts or guarantees.
